# Text: paragraphs, fonts and their drawing

Status: v1, Sprint 1, T046 parts 2 (processor) and 3 (card, order t046g), T048 (the scripts of section 12, order t048; the emoji of section 12.4 and the font collections of section 2.6, order t048e), T049 (the spans of section 6.5, order t049), and T050 (the attributes of section 6.6, order t050). Owner of the rules: the maintainer, under `CLAUDE.md` section 1 (a composition is plain files; animation and drawing are ours) and `PLAN.md` section 3 (the crate `text`, and the two drawing renderers). The notation's rules for text are `docs/spec/notation.md`, sections 6.4, 6.5, 6.12, 7.3, N1, N2, N6, Q5 and the messages of section 13; where this file says a rule is ours, it is stated as such.

## 1. What this version draws

A `<text>` is one paragraph. Its words, and those of the `<span>`s inside it (section 6.5), are its text, and it is drawn on the processor's renderer (`draw-cpu`) with:

- the family, size, weight, style, colour, alignment, line height and letter spacing of section 7.3 of the notation, for the characters that the declared families map (section 2.4): Latin, Cyrillic, Arabic, Hebrew, Japanese and Thai, each shaped, ordered and broken as section 12 states;
- a width that wraps its lines, or none, in which case it is one line as wide as its text; a `max-width` that wraps its lines and leaves it as wide as its widest line (section 5.3);
- its place as an item of a row or a column (`render-command.md`, section 4.3, rule 14), with `align-items` and `align-self`, including `baseline`, which lines up the baselines of the first lines of the texts in a row (section 8);
- the inheritance of the text attributes from the boxes that hold it (section 8).
- the upper case, the kinds of figures, the shadows, the outline and the choice not to wrap of section 6.6.

Not in this version, and reported by `framesmith check` and refused by `framesmith render` before the first frame, with the element's line (FS-0035, or FS-0056 where a bullet names it):

- a family that no `<font>` of the composition declares, including the default `sans-serif`, `serif`, `monospace` and every family of the machine (section 2.3);
- a character that none of the declared families of the list has (section 2.4); a glyph of a colour picture that section 12.4 does not draw is refused with FS-0056 instead, on the line of its `<text>`;
- a font file that is not a TrueType font, such as an OpenType font with PostScript outlines or a WOFF2 file (section 2.1), and a font collection that section 2.6 does not read;
- a `<span>` attribute that section 6.5 does not draw, a `direction` attribute (S21 of the notation), and a text attribute beyond those of section 6.4 that this version draws (section 6.4 below);
- a `<span>` with a margin or a padding in a `<text>` that has no word, or only white space (section 6.5, rule 9), reported on the line of the `<text>`;
- a `<span>` with a margin or a padding whose room falls where a line may end before the span, after a character other than a space or a hyphen (section 6.5, rule 5, and section 10), reported on the line of its attribute;
- an absolute `<text>` with `left` and `right` and no `width`, in a row, a column or a set of columns (section 8);

## 2. Fonts

### 2.1 The `<font>` element and the files it names

A `<font src="..." family="...">` stands in the root (notation 6.12). Its `src` is taken by `Project::resolve` (T032): a path written from the project folder, or, when it begins with `/`, a path on the machine that is read as it is. Its file is read in full and must be a TrueType font (its first four bytes are `0x00010000` or the tag `true`) or a font collection (its first four bytes are the tag `ttcf`, read as section 2.6 says). A file whose first four bytes are the tag `OTTO` (OpenType with PostScript outlines) or `wOF2` (WOFF2) is a font of a kind this version does not draw: it is unsupported (FS-0035). Any other file is not a font: it is invalid (FS-0051, exit code 1). A `src` that names no file, at the path it gives from the project folder or on the machine, is reported with FS-0049; a file that exists and cannot be read is reported with FS-0050, a failure. Each report carries the element's line and the path as written.

The face of a file is the one of the font; a collection gives the one face that section 2.6 chooses. Two `<font>` elements with the same `src` and `family` name the same face and are used once (notation 6.12). Two files of one family name are two faces of one family; the test font of decision A8 is such a pair (Regular, weight class 400, and Bold, weight class 700, under the same family).

### 2.2 The family name of a face

A `<font>` with a `family` names its face by that name. Without one, the family is the first family name the file's own name table gives, as the font library reads it (for the test font, `Framesmith Test Stroke`). The name is kept as written; matching of names is exact, case included.

Source: notation 6.12 (`family` default: the name inside the file); the OpenType specification, the `name` table.

### 2.3 The family list of `font-family`

The value is a list of family names separated by commas, tried in order (notation N1). Every name in the list must be the family of a `<font>` of the composition in this version. A name that is a family of the machine, or the generic `sans-serif`, `serif` or `monospace`, is unsupported; the text refuses with the element's line. An empty entry (`A,,B`) and an empty value are reported as the notation's section 13 states. A text with no `font-family` has the default `sans-serif` and so is unsupported until a `<font>` is named for it.

The reason is not a taste. The rule that draws from the machine's fonts (N2, section 14 of the notation) needs the machine's font interfaces, which are the work of T048 and T052 through frx7's system crates. Until then a family of the machine would be drawn by a font we cannot name, so the text is refused rather than drawn wrong (`CLAUDE.md` section 2: never render a wrong video silently).

### 2.4 Characters

Each character of the text, after the white-space rule of section 5.1, is drawn by the first family of the list that has it (N1). A character that none of the listed families maps is unsupported in this version, for the same reason as section 2.3: N2 would draw it from a font of the machine. The test font maps the printable ASCII characters U+0020 to U+007E and no other (test-font.md, section 2); a text in a fixture that uses the test font uses those characters only. The scripts of section 12 are written in a font the composition declares that maps them, which for those fixtures is a font of the machine named by its path (section 12.1).

### 2.5 Glyphs and shaping

Letters are shaped by the font's own tables through the shaping engine (section 10). The test font has no layout features: no kerning, no ligature and no alternate form. A text in a fixture therefore has the advances the font's `hmtx` table gives, plus the letter spacing of section 6.

### 2.6 Font collections

A font collection is a file whose first four bytes are the tag `ttcf`. It is read for one of its faces, and only that face, as follows. The header and the table directories are those of the OpenType specification (font collections: the TTC header, versions 1.0 and 2.0, which gives the number of faces and the offset from the start of the file of each face's table directory; every table offset of a face is from the start of the file).

1. **The header.** A header, a table directory or a `name` table of a face that lies outside the file, or cannot be read, makes the file not a font: FS-0051, invalid (section 2.1). So does a face whose `name` table gives no family name at all, hidden or not: neither the Macintosh platform nor the Windows platform gives a name with name ID 1 (point 2).
2. **Hidden faces.** A face is hidden when its family name begins with a dot. Its family name is the first family name of the `name` table's entry with name ID 1 for the Macintosh platform (platform ID 1), or, when there is none, for the Windows platform (platform ID 3). A hidden face is not read.
3. **The one face.** A collection must have exactly one face that is not hidden. A collection with none, or with more than one, is unsupported (FS-0035), and the message gives the number of its faces that are not hidden.
4. **Colour pictures only.** That face must have an `sbix` table (section 12.4). A face with outlines and no `sbix` table is unsupported (FS-0035): a collection is drawn in this version for its colour pictures only.
5. **The name.** The face is named by its family name, as section 2.2 names a face without a `family`. A `<font>` with a `family` on a collection must give that name; any other name is unsupported (FS-0035) on the line of the `<font>`.
6. **What is read.** The engine is given the bytes of the file and the index of the one face, so no other face of the file is read or drawn.

The refusals of points 1, 3, 4 and 5 of this section, of point 3 of section 12.4 (the graphics `dupe` and `flip`) and of point 9 of section 12.4 (frx7's refusal of a picture) are tested by `crates/cli/tests/font_collection_refusals.rs`, each by `framesmith check` and by `framesmith render`: the code, the reason in the message and the line, the same report from both, and no film from `render`. The collections of those tests are made from the bytes of the project's test font.

Source: the OpenType specification, the font collection header and the table directory (section 11).

## 3. Font size, weight and style

### 3.1 Size

`font-size` is a number of pixels above 0 (N6, notation 7.3); the default is 16. Zero or a negative value is reported with the element's line as `` `font-size="0"` must be above 0 `` (FS-0053, invalid, the wording of the notation's section 13). The composition is refused with exit code 1 and no video is written. A font size of zero is reported, never drawn: this is the rule criterion t046-c8 holds.

### 3.2 Weight: which face is drawn

`font-weight` is a number (the default 400, notation 7.3). Any finite number is taken as written; a word such as `bold` is reported as FS-0032, the notation's "is not a number" (section 13). The face is chosen from the faces of the family by the font-weight matching of CSS Fonts Level 4, section 5.2, which is the rule of the font library the shaper uses:

- a desired weight from 400 to 500 inclusive: the faces from the desired weight up to 500 in ascending order, then the faces below the desired weight in descending order, then the faces above 500 in ascending order;
- a desired weight below 400: the faces at or below it in descending order, then the faces above it in ascending order;
- a desired weight above 500: the faces at or above it in ascending order, then the faces below it in descending order.

With the test font (weights 400 and 700): 400 and 500 draw the Regular face; 600, 700, 800 and 900 draw the Bold face; 300 draws the Regular face.

**Our rule, not CSS's:** no synthetic bold. CSS may embolden a face to a weight it does not have; the notation has no attribute for that, so the face is drawn as it is, with no emboldening, for every weight. A weight heavier than every face of the family draws the heaviest face. This is a known difference from browsers, stated here.

### 3.3 Style

`font-style` is `normal` or `italic`. A face of the family with an italic style is drawn for `italic`. Without one, `italic` is an oblique: the upright face is sheared about the baseline by 14 degrees, which is the default angle of the oblique style in CSS Fonts Level 4. The shear moves a point of a glyph at height `h` above the baseline to the right by `h × tan 14°`; the advances are not changed. The shear is applied to the glyph outlines, not to the layout.

## 4. Line height

`line-height` is `normal`, a number, or a number with `px` (notation 7.3).

- **`normal`**: the line's natural height from the font's metrics. The ascender and the descender are the typographic ones when the font's OS/2 `fsSelection` has USE_TYPO_METRICS (bit 7) set; otherwise the `hhea` ones. The leading is added to the height (the line gap of the same table). This is the rule of the font library, which follows the usual reading of the OpenType specification (`OS/2`, `hhea`). For the test font, both tables give an ascender of 850, a descender of −250 and a line gap of 0, so `normal` is 1.1 times the font size at every size.
- **A number**: that many times the font size. `1.5` at 100 pixels is 150 pixels.
- **A number with `px`**: that many pixels. A value of 0 or less is reported with FS-0053 (N6). Any other form, such as a percentage, is reported with FS-0052.

Within a line the baseline sits at the line's top, plus half of the difference between the line height and the ascender plus descender, plus the ascender. This is the half-leading rule of CSS Inline Layout Level 3. Consecutive baselines are one line height apart. The first line's top is the top of the text.

## 5. Lines

### 5.1 White space

A run of spaces, tabs and line ends between words is one space; white space at the start and the end of the text is dropped (notation 7.3). The rule is applied before shaping, so a tab or a line end of the source is a space. A no-break space (U+00A0) is not in the test font and is unsupported (section 2.4).

### 5.2 Where a line may end

A line may end where the Unicode line-breaking rules allow (UAX 14) and nowhere else. The data is the line-break data of Unicode 15.1.0. The segmenter is the engine's dictionary segmenter (section 12.3, the `complex-scripts` feature of section 10), which uses a word dictionary for the scripts that have one and the rules of UAX 14 for the rest. The options are the engine's defaults. For Latin, Cyrillic, Hebrew and Arabic text the line ends at a space, and after a hyphen, as UAX 14 gives.

A word wider than its line is not broken (notation 7.3), as CSS's `overflow-wrap: normal` gives it (decision B19 of `DECISIONS.md`, 2026-10-17). It stays whole on a line of its own, and that line runs past the width. Criterion t046-c3 is judged on a text that has places to break: there no line is wider than the width, except a line that holds one such word and nothing else.

A width of 0 wraps at every place a line may end (notation N6): each word is then a line of its own.

### 5.3 Width of a line and of the text

A line's width does not count the space it ends at, nor the letter spacing of that space (notation 7.3). The width of the text, when it has a `width`, is that width. Without one the text is as wide as its widest line. The line's position across the text is given by `text-align` (section 6.4).

With a `max-width` (notation 7.1) and no `width` of its own, the text breaks its lines so that no line is wider than the `max-width`, at the places the line-breaking rules of section 5.2 allow, and it is as wide as its widest line: a text shorter than the `max-width` keeps its own width. A word that is wider than the `max-width` has no such place, so it is kept whole and its line is wider than the `max-width` (decision B19, `DECISIONS.md`). A `width` of the text's own makes the `max-width` have no effect. The alignment of section 6.2 is within the widest line.

### 5.4 Height of the text

The height of the text is the sum of its line heights (section 4), unless a `height` is written; then the box is that high, and the lines start at its top.

## 6. Letter spacing and alignment

### 6.1 Letter spacing

`letter-spacing` is a number of pixels, or a number with `em` that is multiplied by the font size of the element that writes it, so a child that inherits the value takes it in pixels, as CSS does. A negative value is allowed (N6). Any other form is reported with FS-0052. A letter is a typographic character unit (CSS Text Level 4, letter-spacing): an extended grapheme cluster of UAX 29, which for the text of this version is one printable ASCII character, a space included. The spacing is added after every letter, the last letter of each line included, so a line of `n` letters is wider by `n` times the spacing.

The space that ends a wrapped line is the exception: neither its advance nor its spacing counts toward the line's width (section 5.3).

This is the rule of the shaping engine (spacing after each atom, as Blink applies it). The engine's alignment counts the trailing space of an unwrapped line, but no unwrapped line ends in a space, because of the white-space rule (section 5.1).

### 6.2 Alignment

`text-align` is `left`, `center` or `right` (notation 7.3); any other word is reported as FS-0039, the notation's "not one of" (section 13). Each line lies at that side of the text's width: its left edge at 0, at `(width − line width) / 2`, or at `width − line width`, where the line width is that of section 5.3. For a text with no `width` every line is as wide as the text, so the alignment has no effect.

### 6.3 Colour

`color` is a colour (notation 7.3, `#rrggbb` or `#rrggbbaa`). A text whose colour is written with an alpha below `ff` is refused as unsupported in this version: the colours of the picture have no alpha (the existing colour type of the drawing crate). The colour of a text that names none is black, `#000000`, fully opaque (Q5 of the notation, a maintainer draft).

### 6.4 `text-align`, and the other attributes of this version

The text attributes of this version are: `font-family`, `font-weight`, `font-style`, `font-size`, `line-height`, `letter-spacing`, `color` and `text-align`, and the five of section 6.6: `text-transform`, `white-space`, `font-variant-numeric`, `text-shadow` and `text-stroke`. Every other attribute of the `<text>` table of notation 6.4 that is not a placement (section 8) is unsupported.

### 6.5 Spans

A `<span>` is a piece of the text of the `<text>` it stands in, or of the span that holds it (`notation.md`, section 6.5). Its words are part of the paragraph: they sit on the lines of the paragraph, break where section 5.2 allows, across the edges of the span as well, and are ordered with the rest of their line by section 12.2. A span holds words and `<span>`s; a `<tween>` or a `<set>` inside a span is refused, as section 7 refuses one inside a text.

1. **Text attributes.** A span starts from the text attributes of its parent, as section 8 gives them, and changes those it writes. Its words are drawn with `font-family`, `font-weight`, `font-style`, `font-size`, `line-height`, `letter-spacing` and `color` as it has them. `white-space` and `text-transform` are read as section 6.4 says. `id` and `class` are names (notation N7).
2. **Size and line height.** A span's `font-size` is the size of its own glyphs. Its `line-height` is read as section 4 says, with the span's own size: `normal` is the line height of its own font, and a number is that many times its size. The pieces of a line all stand on one baseline, as the inline boxes of a line do in CSS Inline Layout Level 3 (`vertical-align: baseline`). So a span with a larger size makes its line as tall as the larger size needs, and the words beside it stay on the same baseline.
3. **Colour.** Each word is drawn in the colour of the span it is in, which is the colour of its text where the span writes none (section 6.3). The processor and the card draw each glyph run in its own colour (section 9).
4. **Margin and padding.** A span's `margin` and `padding` act on its two sides only, and are read as one to four numbers in pixels, in the order top, right, bottom, left that CSS's shorthand uses. The left space is `margin-left` plus `padding-left`, and lies before the span's first character: where the line breaks at the space before the span, it goes with the span to the line the span begins (section 10). Where the line may end before the span, at a space or after a hyphen, the left space goes with the span to the line the span begins; where no line may end there, it stays with the words before it (section 10). The right space is `margin-right` plus `padding-right`, and lies after its last character. Each space takes room in its line and moves the words after it. A span with no words keeps both its spaces between the words beside it, as CSS keeps the margins of an empty inline box. A space does not change the height of its line, and the top and bottom values have no effect on the layout, as CSS Inline Layout Level 3 gives margin and padding of a non-replaced inline element. Not in `notation.md` in so many words, and so stated here: the notation gives the margin and the padding of a span their meaning from CSS, and this section states how far they reach in this version.
5. **Values refused.** A `margin` or a `padding` that is a percentage is refused with FS-0035. A negative `margin` is refused with FS-0035 (the notation allows it, and this version does not draw it). A negative `padding` is refused with FS-0034, as the `padding` of a box is. Each is reported on the line of its attribute, and the span is not drawn. A margin or a padding whose room falls where the line may end before the span, after a character other than a space or a hyphen, is refused with FS-0035 on the line of its attribute, and the text is not drawn, since no carrier keeps both the line end and the room's place there (section 10). Such a character is, for example, the first of two ideographs, which a line may end between (UAX 14, rule LB31), an em dash (class B2) or a middle dot (class NS).
6. **Attributes not drawn.** A span that writes `text-align`, `text-shadow`, `text-stroke`, `font-variant-numeric`, `start`, `duration`, `typed`, an attribute of `background`, of the look or of the movement of section 8.1 of the notation, or `white-space` or `text-transform` in a value other than `normal` or `none`, is refused with FS-0035 on the line of the attribute, and the text is not drawn. The span's message names `normal` or `none` as the one form a span takes. `text-align` is refused on a span although the table of `notation.md`, section 6.5, lists it. A span has no line of its own to align: CSS gives `text-align` to block containers, and section 6.2 gives the alignment to the lines of the paragraph. This departs from that table. The motion of a span is T069, and the other attributes wait for the ticket that draws them. The attributes of section 6.6 that a `<text>` writes are not written by a span: a span takes them from its text by inheritance (section 6.6, rule 1).
7. **Nesting.** A span inside a span is a piece of the same kind, and its spaces nest: the outer span's left space lies before the inner span's first character, and its right space after the inner span's last character.
8. **Where a line may end.** A span's edges and its spaces are not places where a line may end. A line ends only where section 5.2 allows, and a span with a space at its edge keeps its words on the line the words beside them are on, unless the line-breaking rules put a break there. The break at the space before a span is the one section 5.2 gives at that space, and the room of the span goes with it to the next line (section 10). A room adds no line end: the lines of a text with its room are those of the text without it, and where a carrier could not keep them, the room is refused (section 10).
9. **Room with no word.** A `<text>` whose words are none, or only white space, and that holds a `<span>` with a margin or a padding, is refused with FS-0035 on the line of the `<text>`, and it is not drawn. CSS keeps the room of an empty inline box; this version refuses it instead, because a room with no word beside it has no line of its own to be kept on. Room between words, including the room of a span with no words, is drawn (rule 4).

### 6.6 Upper case, figures, shadows, outline and no wrapping

The five attributes of notation 7.3 that a `<text>` draws: `text-transform`, `white-space`, `font-variant-numeric`, `text-shadow` and `text-stroke`. Sources: notation 7.3 (their meaning and their forms), 7.5 (a list of shadows: the first listed is on top), 7.6 (the blur of a shadow), N6 (the values that are refused below 0), N9 (the forms of a number) and section 13 (the messages); CSS Text Level 3 (`text-transform`, `white-space: nowrap`), CSS Fonts Level 4 (`font-variant-numeric`) and CSS Text Decoration Level 3 (`text-shadow`), for the meanings the notation takes from CSS (`CLAUDE.md` section 1). Where a rule below is ours and not CSS's, it says so.

1. **Inheritance.** Each attribute is taken from the nearest element above the text that writes it, as the other text attributes are (section 8): a `<box>` may write it and its texts take it. A span does not write them (section 6.5, rule 6) and takes them from its `<text>`, so the words of a span are drawn with the attribute of the paragraph. The attribute is read where the element writes it, and the value is checked there (rule 7).
2. **`text-transform`** is `none` or `uppercase`. `uppercase` gives each character of the words of the paragraph its upper-case form under the Unicode default case mapping, before the characters are laid out; a character may become more than one (`ß` becomes `SS`). The words are changed in the copy that is laid out only: the composition keeps the words as the author wrote them. `none` is the default. The lower-case and the capitalising transforms are not in the list and are refused (rule 7).
3. **`white-space`** is `normal` or `nowrap`. `nowrap` makes the paragraph one line: no line ends at a place where section 5.2 allows one, however narrow the `width` or the `max-width` of the text. The line is placed within the width of the text by `text-align`, as section 6.2 places every line; so a line wider than the width starts before the left edge under `center` and `right`, as an overlong word does (section 5.2). This is our rule: CSS starts an overflowing line at its start edge.
4. **`font-variant-numeric`** is `normal`, or a list of the words `lining-nums`, `oldstyle-nums`, `tabular-nums` and `proportional-nums`, separated by spaces. `normal` alone asks for nothing, and `normal` in a list is refused. Each word asks the font for one OpenType feature for the runs of the paragraph: `lining-nums` the feature `lnum`, `oldstyle-nums` the feature `onum`, `tabular-nums` the feature `tnum` and `proportional-nums` the feature `pnum`. Where a list holds both words of a pair (`lining-nums` with `oldstyle-nums`, or `tabular-nums` with `proportional-nums`), the one written last is asked for and the other is dropped. This is our rule: the pairs exclude each other in CSS. A font without the feature asked for shows its own figures (notation 7.3). The test font of decision A8 has the features `onum` and `tnum` (`test-font.md`, section 7): with it `oldstyle-nums` draws the old-style figures and `tabular-nums` the tabular figures, as the fixtures `text-figure-oldstyle` and `text-figure-widths` show. It has no `lnum` and no `pnum`, so `lining-nums` and `proportional-nums` draw its own figures, the lining and proportional ones, as `normal` does.
5. **`text-shadow`** is `none`, or a list of shadows separated by commas. A shadow is two lengths, the offset across (`X`) and the offset down (`Y`), with an optional blur and an optional colour, in either order, as CSS Text Decoration Level 3 reads the `text-shadow` list. A length is a number of pixels, with an optional `px` after it (N9 gives the form of the number). A shadow without a colour takes the colour of its text, as CSS's `currentcolor` does. A colour is written as section 6.3 writes one.
   - **What is drawn.** The alpha of the glyph fills of the paragraph is moved by `X` and `Y`, blurred, and laid in the shadow's colour under the glyphs. The alpha is that of the glyph fills and of the colour pictures of section 12.4, so an emoji casts its shadow too; the outline of rule 6 casts none. The glyph fills move by `X` and `Y` as they are written. A colour picture moves to the whole pixel nearest to its moved top-left corner, as section 12.4 places one. Only the pixels of the text's clip change (section 9).
   - **The blur.** The blur is the method of notation 7.6 with a standard deviation `sigma` of half the blur: three passes, each over a span of `floor(sigma * 3 * sqrt(2 * pi) / 4 + 0.5)` pixels, where a span below 2 leaves the shadow as it is. A pass averages across the picture and then down it. Each pixel becomes the average of the span of pixels that runs from `(span - 1) / 2` (rounded down) before it to `span - 1 - (span - 1) / 2` after it, and a pixel outside the frame counts as clear. Each average is rounded to the nearest whole level, a half rounded up. A blur of 0 draws the shadow sharp. This is our reading of notation 7.6 for a shadow of text: 7.6 gives the method for a picture as a whole, and does not say where the span of a pixel is centred or how a half is rounded.
   - **The order.** The first listed shadow is on top (notation 7.5), so the last listed is laid first and each shadow is laid over those listed after it, all under the glyphs.
6. **`text-stroke`** is a width and a colour, `width colour`, as notation 7.3 gives it and as `border` gives its line. The width is a number of pixels, with an optional `px`, and is 0 or more (N6); a width of 0 draws no outline. The outline is a line of that width, centred on the outline of each glyph (half of it lies outside the glyph and half inside), in that colour. It is laid over the glyph fills and under the colour pictures of emoji, which have no outline. The colour is written as section 6.3 writes one.
7. **Forms refused.** Each is reported on the line of its attribute, with the value as written and the values taken (notation section 13), and the text is not drawn:
   - `text-transform` other than `none` or `uppercase`: `` `text-transform="lowercase"` is not one of: none, uppercase `` (FS-0039);
   - `white-space` other than `normal` or `nowrap`: `` `white-space="pre"` is not one of: normal, nowrap `` (FS-0039);
   - `font-variant-numeric` that is not `normal` or a list of the four words, or that holds `normal` with other words: `` `font-variant-numeric="slashed-zero"` is not `normal` or a list of: lining-nums, oldstyle-nums, tabular-nums, proportional-nums `` (FS-0039);
   - `text-shadow` that is not `none` or a list of shadows of the form of rule 5: `` `text-shadow="..."` is not a list of shadows `` (FS-0052);
   - `text-stroke` that is not a width and a colour: `` `text-stroke="..."` is not a width and a colour `` (FS-0052);
   - a blur below 0 or a width below 0: `` `text-shadow="..."` must be 0 or more `` or `` `text-stroke="..."` must be 0 or more `` (FS-0034, N6);
   - a colour with an alpha below `ff`: `` `text-shadow="..."` has an alpha below ff, which this version does not draw `` (FS-0035, section 6.3).
8. **Renderers.** Both renderers draw the five attributes. The processor draws the shadows and the outline of a paragraph as pictures of the frame, and the card places those pictures in the same order, under and over its glyphs (`card-renderer.md`, section 9.9). The card makes no glyph mask for them, so the masks of section 9.6 of that file are unchanged by these attributes.
9. **Not in this version.** A figure kind other than the four words; the lower-case and the capitalising transforms (T050's out of scope); an outline that is dashed or painted in another order; a shadow that spreads, an inset shadow, and the shadow of a whole box (T055); a span that writes one of the five attributes; a text that moves or changes size on the card, since no `<tween>` acts on a text (section 7).

## 7. Animation

A text takes no `<tween>` or `<set>` in this version. Animated colour and size of text are Sprint 4 (the ticket's out-of-scope list); a `<tween>` or `<set>` in a `<text>` is unsupported.

## 8. Placement, and the inheritance of text attributes

A `<text>` is placed by the rules of placement of section 7.1 of the notation (`left`, `top`, `right`, `bottom`, `position`, `margin`, `width`, `height`, `max-width`), exactly as a box is placed (T041). Its `start` and `duration` hold it to a window as for a box (T042).

In a row or a column a `<text>` is an item of the flow (`render-command.md`, section 4.3, rule 14). Its size is that of its text: a text with no `width` is as wide as its widest line, and one with no `height` is as high as its lines (section 5.4), each rounded up to a whole pixel; its `max-width`, `margin`, `align-self` and `flex-grow` act as for a box. It is not stretched across: `stretch` leaves it at its own size and at the start edge. Its baseline, for `align-items="baseline"` in a row, is the baseline of its first line from its top (section 4), so two texts of different sizes in a row share the baseline of their first lines. A `<text>` that is `position="absolute"` is placed by its edges, as a box is. A percentage `width` or `height` of a `<text>` in a row or a column is not drawn in this version: it is reported by `framesmith check` and refused by `framesmith render` with FS-0035 on the line of the attribute, as an attribute this version does not draw. An absolute `<text>` in a row, a column or a set of columns that has `left` and `right` and no `width` is not drawn in this version either: `framesmith check` reports it and `framesmith render` refuses it with FS-0035 on the line of the `<text>`. The percentage case above is reported on the line of its attribute instead. Every other absolute `<text>` in them is placed by its edges as this section says.

The text attributes of section 3 to 6 pass from a box to everything inside it, and from a text to its children, unless the child writes its own value. A child that writes one wins (criterion t046-c7). At the root the defaults of the table apply. A box takes no `overflow` in this version and `framesmith render` refuses it with FS-0035; `framesmith check` reports it only when a `<style>` gives it (`check-command.md`, section 3c). So no box clips a text: a text is drawn inside its canvas only. A `<text>` with a `background`, a `padding` or another attribute of section 6.4 that this version does not draw is unsupported: `framesmith check` and `framesmith render` both report it with FS-0035 on the line of the attribute, as they report an element inside a `<text>` (section 1).

## 9. Renderers and refusals

Both renderers draw text. The processor's renderer (`draw-cpu`, name `processor`) draws a paragraph by its glyph runs, each in its own colour (`render-command.md`, section 4; section 6.5). The card's renderer (`draw-gpu`, name `card`) draws the same paragraphs, by the method of `card-renderer.md` section 9, and its picture agrees with the processor's within the tolerance of `card-renderer.md` section 4. The shadows and the outline of section 6.6 are drawn by the processor as pictures, and the card places them (`card-renderer.md`, section 9.9). So `--backend gpu`, and `--backend auto` with a card, draw a composition with a `<text>`, and `framesmith check` and `framesmith render` agree on it.

A renderer that draws no text refuses a composition with a `<text>` before any frame and before the film is opened: the report of FS-0035 on the line of the first `<text>`, the closing line of an unsupported form, and exit code 2. The rule is kept for a renderer that does not draw text, and its test uses such a renderer (`crates/cli/tests/text_refused_by_a_renderer_without_text.rs`). It is not a silent fallback (T040): no renderer is swapped for another. The check is made after the renderer is chosen and started, so a `gpu` without a card is still refused with FS-0038 first.

With `--backend auto` the card is chosen when it starts (`render-command.md`, section 1.2), and it draws the text on the card. The processor is chosen only when no card starts, and it draws the text too.

## 10. The shaping and line-breaking engine

The text crate (`crates/text`) draws its paragraphs with the shaping engine parley, version 0.12.0, with its default features off (`Cargo.toml`, the `parley` line of the workspace) and with its feature `complex-scripts` on, which the crate's own `Cargo.toml` turns on: the engine then breaks lines and words with its dictionary segmenters (section 12.3). The feature adds no crate to the lock file. The build holds no platform font backend, since parley's `system` feature is left out, so no option of the font collection reads a font of the machine. The tests of `crates/text/tests/no_font_backend_of_the_system_is_built.rs` hold this: the lock file, which lists the dependencies of every system, has no font interface of a system, and the engine's font library depends on none of them; with the `system` feature turned on they fail. The test `a_new_collection_lists_no_family_and_resolves_no_family_of_the_machine` (`crates/text/tests/unknown_families_and_characters.rs`) asks the engine's own collection, which a new context leaves with no family. The engine's own font library, the shaper and the segmenter are its dependencies and are named in section 11. The card's glyph masks use the same rasteriser as the processor, vello_cpu 0.3.0, through the calls of `card-renderer.md` section 9; no other engine draws text on the card. No type of the engine is in a public interface of our crates.

A span's margin and padding are reserved in its line as room, which is spacing and not an inline box: each room adds its width to the letter spacing of the character before it, so the words after it move on by that width (section 6.5, rule 4). Room with no character before it is carried by a carrier, as the next paragraph states. The engine ends a line after every in-flow inline box, whatever the break classes around it: measured with the test font, `AB`, an inline box of 30 pixels and `CD` in a width that holds `AB` alone broke into three lines, the box alone on the middle one. An inline box therefore cannot hold room between two letters, which rule 8 forbids.

Room is carried by a carrier where no character holds it: a character of the engine's that is placed in the text, shaped at font size 0 and line height 0, so that it has no advance and no height, and whose letter spacing is the room. A run at font size 0 is a carrier run. It is skipped before the check for a missing character, and it is left out of the drawn glyphs, so a carrier is never counted as missing and never drawn, whatever the font declares; no font size of a composition is 0 (section 3.1).

The carrier of each room is chosen from the line breaks that the text has without the room. Those are the places where the engine's line breaker may end a line, applying the line-breaking rules of Unicode Standard Annex 14 (UAX 14) to the text as it is when it is broken at width 0, where every place a line may end is taken (section 5.2). Each character has a small letter spacing for that run, so that none has an advance of 0 and hides a break. For each room, with the character before its place (none at the start of a text), the carrier is:

- none at the start of a text: a word joiner (U+2060), placed first in the text. It allows no break on either side (LB11);
- where no line may end at the place, and a letter or a digit before it: no carrier, and the room is spacing after that character (section 6.5, rule 4);
- where no line may end at the place, and any other character before it: a word joiner (LB11). A space before a full stop is such a place, since a full stop is kept with the space before it (LB13), and so is a hyphen before a digit, which LB25 keeps together, and a place before a hyphen or a class BA character, which LB21 never breaks before;
- where a line may end at the place: a no-break space (U+00A0), placed after the character before it, with the room as its letter spacing. A line may end after a space, which LB18 breaks after, and after a hyphen that LB25 does not keep with a digit. A break may come before a no-break space that follows a space, a class BA or a class HY character (LB12a), and no break may follow a no-break space (LB12). So the line still ends where it does without the room, and the room goes with the span to the line the span begins. After any other character, LB12a forbids the break before the no-break space, and the room is refused (section 6.5, rule 5). Such a character is the first of two ideographs, which a line may end between (LB31), an em dash (class B2) or a middle dot (class NS).

The carried text is broken again at width 0, and its places are mapped back to the characters of the text without carriers. A room is accepted only where the two sets of places are equal; a place that a carrier moved or removed is the refusal of the room that holds it. The rules above choose the carrier, and this comparison checks that the carrier kept the break.

The no-break space was chosen by a break table, measured with the test font at 24 pixels and a width of 60 for the text `AB `, one character, and `CD`. With no character after the space the line breaks at the space, into lines of 32.76 and 30.67 pixels. With a zero-width non-joiner (U+200C), a word joiner (U+2060) or a zero-width joiner (U+200D) after the space the line does not break there, so the room could not go to the second line. With a no-break space the line breaks at the space, into lines of 32.76 and 37.87 pixels, and not after the no-break space. A zero-width space (U+200B) and a soft hyphen (U+00AD) break after themselves, and a byte order mark (U+FEFF) holds the line whole; none of these is used. A carrier does not change the height of a line.

Known limits of the room carriers: none. A room where a line may end and no carrier keeps the line end is refused, at any width: the places are those of the text at width 0, so a text written with no width, which breaks nowhere, is refused the same way, since its container may give it a narrower width. The results are tested in `crates/text/tests/room_carriers_and_line_breaks.rs` (a room after a hyphen at a break keeps the break and moves the span's letter to 12 pixels on the second line, `room_after_a_hyphen_at_a_break_is_drawn_at_the_start_of_the_line_the_span_begins`; a room after a hyphen before a digit and a room before a full stop after a space keep the lines of the text without the room, `room_after_a_hyphen_before_a_digit_keeps_the_lines_of_the_text_without_it` and `a_room_before_a_full_stop_after_a_space_keeps_the_lines_of_the_text_without_it`), in `crates/text/tests/room_refused_at_other_line_breaks.rs` (the refusals after two ideographs and after an em dash, and a room of 0 that is not refused, drawn with the machine's font, so compiled for macOS only), and in `crates/cli/tests/check_and_render_agree_on_span_refusals.rs` (`check_and_render_refuse_a_room_between_two_ideographs_on_the_line_of_its_attribute`, the report of `check` and `render` on the line of the margin). The pictures are the checked-in fixtures `text-span-room-at-a-hyphen` and `text-span-room-before-a-full-stop`.

The colour of a span is the brush of its style in the engine, and each glyph run carries the colour of its style. The engine's behaviour this spec relies on was read from the source of the libraries named in section 11 and measured by a scratch program outside the repository (not kept): the weight matching of section 3.2, the oblique of section 3.3, the line height of section 4, the spacing of section 6.1 and the hanging space of section 5.3. The values measured for the test font at 100 pixels were: `normal` line height 110, so baselines 110 apart at `normal` (the rule of section 4 and the test `the_normal_line_height_is_the_font_size_times_one_point_one` of `crates/text/tests/line_height.rs`); baselines 150 apart at `1.5`; 40 apart at `40px`; `AB` with a letter spacing of 10 widens by 20; the weight 500 face is the Regular face and 600 is the Bold face.

## 12. Scripts: fonts, shaping, order, breaks and emoji

This section states what the scripts of the ticket T048 do: Cyrillic, Arabic, Hebrew, Japanese, Thai and emoji, with Latin as before. Their pictures are judged by behaviour, on macOS only, and not byte for byte (decision A9). Sources: section 11.

### 12.1 The fonts of the scripts

The fixtures of the scripts (section 12.5) draw their scripts with the machine's font `Arial Unicode`, named by its path in the composition: `<font src="/System/Library/Fonts/Supplemental/Arial Unicode.ttf" family="Arial Unicode"/>`. Its file is read as it is (section 2.1), and its family is the one the `<font>` element declares, so `font-family="Arial Unicode"` names a family of the composition (section 2.3). The file is a TrueType font. It maps the Latin, Cyrillic, Hebrew, Thai, Japanese kana and kanji characters, and most Arabic letters; it maps no emoji (section 12.4). Because the file belongs to the machine, the tests that read it are compiled for macOS only, and no test of another system reads it (decision A9).

In this version a character that the named font lacks is refused, as section 2.4 says. The rule that draws such a character from the machine's font is T052's; until then it is a known gap (decision A9).

### 12.2 Shaping, order and direction

1. Shaping. A run of text in one script and one direction is shaped by the engine with the font's own tables, with the features the font gives for the script. The letters of Arabic take the contextual forms the font gives them (the `init`, `medi`, `fina` and `isol` forms of GSUB). So a letter inside a word is drawn with the glyph of its joined form, which is not the glyph the font has for that letter alone.
2. Base direction. The base direction of a paragraph is left to right in this version, for every script. The notation has no attribute for the direction (S21 of the notation), and the proofs took the same rule (`_swe/poc/tree/docs/spec/native-notation.md`, section 7, a record only). The runs of a paragraph are put in visual order by the bidirectional algorithm of UAX 9 for that base direction. Each line is drawn in that order.
3. What follows. A run of Arabic or Hebrew letters is drawn from its last letter at the left to its first letter at the right. A run of Latin letters keeps its own order. In a line that mixes them, the runs are placed in the order UAX 9 gives for a left-to-right base: a right-to-left run stays between the runs that come before and after it in the text, and is drawn reversed. A line of Arabic or Hebrew still starts at the left edge of the text; a start at the right edge is S21's, not this version's.

### 12.3 Where a line may end

1. Latin, Cyrillic, Hebrew and Arabic text ends a line at a space, and after a hyphen, as UAX 14 gives (classes SP, HY and AL). The rule of section 5.2 applies: a word wider than its line is kept whole.
2. Japanese text has no spaces. A line may end between any two characters where UAX 14 allows it. It never ends before a character of a class that may not start a line: the closing marks (such as `。`, `、`, `」` and `）`, class CL and CP) and the other classes that rule LB13 keeps with the character before them. It never ends after an opening bracket (such as `「` or `（`, class OP; rule LB14).
3. Thai text has no spaces between its words. A line may end at a space, or at a place that the dictionary of the segmenter gives as a boundary between two words. It never ends inside a word of the dictionary (a line that breaks there is wrong).
4. These places come from the engine's dictionary segmenter, the `complex-scripts` feature of section 10. Without that feature the Thai text of this section would have no place to break, and the Japanese text would be broken by the class rules alone.

### 12.4 Emoji

An emoji is drawn in its own colours, whatever the colour of its text (criterion t048-c6). It is drawn from the colour pictures of the font that holds it, and never from its outlines: a glyph of such a face is drawn only as its picture, or it is refused (point 9).

1. **The face.** A character that a declared family maps (section 2.4) is drawn by the family that maps it. The emoji face is one face of a font collection that section 2.6 reads: a face with an `sbix` table, whose glyphs are colour pictures. The fixtures name the Mac's emoji collection by its path (section 12.5), and list it after the Latin family.
2. **The strike.** The `sbix` table holds strikes, one for each size in pixels per em (`ppem`), and each strike holds the data of the glyphs it has. For a size `s` drawn (section 3.1), the strike of a glyph is the one with the smallest `ppem` that is at least `s`, among the strikes in which the glyph has a picture of the type `png ` (point 3). When there is none, it is the strike with the largest `ppem`. The Mac's emoji font has nine strikes, of 20, 26, 32, 40, 48, 52, 64, 96 and 160 pixels per em (measured on 2026-10-18 with a scratch program outside the repository, not kept). So a size of 20 uses the strike of 20, a size of 30 the strike of 32, and a size of 200 the strike of 160.
3. **The graphic.** The data of a glyph in a strike is a header of 8 bytes (two signed 16-bit origin offsets, and a tag of 4 bytes, the graphic type) and the graphic. This version draws the type `png ` only: the graphic is a PNG file. Every other type is refused (point 9): the type `dupe` of the OpenType specification, which names another glyph, and the type `flip`, which the OpenType specification does not define and which the Mac's emoji font has for 108 glyphs in each strike (measured as in point 2). A glyph with no data in a strike has no picture there. The refusal of the `dupe` and `flip` graphics is tested by `crates/cli/tests/font_collection_refusals.rs`, by check and by render.
4. **Decoding.** The PNG bytes are handed to frx7 to be read from memory, by the codec registry's reader of stills from memory, which gives the picture as straight RGBA with the alpha it stores. Picture decoding is frx7's. A picture that frx7 refuses is refused (point 9), with frx7's reason.
5. **Scale.** For a strike of `ppem` `p` drawn at size `s`, the scale is `k = s / p`. The picture's target size is `round(w x k)` by `round(h x k)` pixels, rounded half up, where `w` and `h` are the picture's size. A target with no pixel in a side draws nothing. Its colours are first premultiplied: a colour `c` with alpha `a` becomes `round(c x a / 255)`. A picture of its own target size is not resampled. Any other picture is resampled to the target size by frx7's scaler (Mitchell cubic, per channel, as frx7's own scaler spec gives it, section 1, a record of our own), and after that each colour is limited to its alpha, since the filter may overshoot. The premultiplied pixels are the picture that is drawn.
6. **Placement.** Let `u` be the `unitsPerEm` of the face (the `head` table) and `m = s / u`. The glyph's pen position is its origin `(x, y)`: `x` where the glyph starts (section 2.5) and `y` its line's baseline (section 4), with y growing downwards. When the glyph's record in `glyf` is not empty, that is, the glyph has contours, the design origin of the picture is the lower-left corner of the glyph's bounding box, which is `(x + xMin x m, y - yMin x m)`, the box being read from the record's header. Otherwise the design origin is `(x, y)`. The picture's lower-left corner is the design origin moved by `(originOffsetX x m, -originOffsetY x m)`, where the origin offsets are the two signed 16-bit values of the glyph's data, in design units: the OpenType specification places the left edge of the graphic `originOffsetX` units to the right of the origin, and its bottom edge `originOffsetY` units above it. Its top-left corner is the lower-left corner moved up by the target height of point 5. When drawn, the top-left corner is placed at the whole pixel nearest to it, halves up, in each direction. The glyph's advance is the one the face's `hmtx` table gives (section 2.5), and the letter spacing of section 6.1 follows it, as it follows any letter. The picture does not change either.
7. **Colour.** The picture keeps its own colours and its own alpha. The colour of its text and of its span does not apply to it, so no colour is written over it. A text has no alpha in its colour (section 6.3), so no opacity of the text applies to it either.
8. **Line.** The baseline and the height of its line are the ones the engine gives the line (section 4). A picture does not change them.
9. **Blank glyphs and refusals.** A glyph of a whitespace character (section 5.1) draws nothing and is not refused. Any other glyph of such a face is refused with FS-0056 when it has no picture of the type `png ` in any strike (points 2 and 3), or when frx7 refuses its picture (point 4). Such a glyph is never drawn from its outlines, in another colour, or in another font. The message names the character by its code point and gives the reason. The refusal is reported on the line of the `<text>` by `framesmith check`, and `framesmith render` refuses it with the same report before the first frame, writing no film (section 9). The refusals of this point are tested by `crates/cli/tests/font_collection_refusals.rs`, by check and by render: the `dupe` and `flip` graphics, and frx7's refusal of a picture.
10. **Both renderers.** The processor (`draw-cpu`) draws a paragraph's pictures after its glyph runs, in the order of the text, into the paragraph's layer, by the src-over operation of the processor's composition on premultiplied pixels. The card (`draw-gpu`) draws each picture as one quad of its pixels, at its whole-pixel place, blended over the frame premultiplied, with each pixel read without filtering (`card-renderer.md`, section 9.8). The two pictures agree within the tolerance of `card-renderer.md`, section 4.

### 12.5 The fixtures of the scripts

Each fixture is a one-second composition at 10 frames a second, on a white canvas of 320 by 300 pixels, with one `<text>` of 300 pixels' width at 20 pixels, a line height of twice the size, black, in the Arial Unicode font of section 12.1. The pictures are judged at frames 0 and 9, which are the same picture, since nothing moves.

| Fixture | Script | Judged by |
|---|---|---|
| `text-cyrillic` | Cyrillic | the letters in reading order; the line breaks at spaces inside the width |
| `text-arabic` | Arabic | the letters drawn right to left; the joined forms; the line breaks at word boundaries |
| `text-hebrew-latin` | Hebrew and Latin in one paragraph | each run in its own direction; the runs in UAX 9 order |
| `text-japanese` | Japanese | the breaks between characters; no line starts with a closing mark |
| `text-thai` | Thai, with no spaces | the breaks at word boundaries only |
| `text-emoji` | an emoji in a line of Latin, in a text of a colour far from its own | the emoji at its place in the line, in its own colours whatever the colour of the text; the line breaks at spaces inside the width (section 12.4) |

The script fixtures have no checked-in pictures (decision A9): a judgement by behaviour is made on the frames the processor draws. The frames of every script fixture, `text-emoji` among them, are judged by behaviour: the lines, the order of the letters, the joined forms, the breaks, the place of the emoji between the words it sits between, and the colours of the emoji against the colour of the text. The judgements are on macOS only, since the fixtures read the machine's fonts.
The card's pictures agree with the processor's on every frame of each fixture, the frames 0 and 9 among them, the emoji fixture included, at the tolerance of `PLAN.md`, section 5 (`card-renderer.md`, sections 4 and 7). That test needs a graphics card, so it is reference machine only. The `check` and `render` commands accept the six fixtures, which name the machine's fonts, and refuse a glyph that has no picture this version draws with FS-0056 on the line of its `<text>`, writing no film (`crates/cli/tests/script_fixtures_on_the_processor.rs`, and, for the refusals of section 2.6 and of points 3 and 9 of section 12.4, `crates/cli/tests/font_collection_refusals.rs`).
