# Hindenburg, Thirty-Four Seconds

A 68-second history film in English about the loss of the airship Hindenburg at Lakehurst, New Jersey, on 6 May 1937.

It opens on a map that moves in to Lakehurst, shows the newsreel of the ship arriving and of the first flame, and counts the 34 seconds it took to burn. It then explains why the ship flew on hydrogen: Hugo Eckener wanted helium, the United States held almost all of it and did not export it, so the ship carried seven million cubic feet of hydrogen. A diagram of the landing field shows the late arrival, the circuit flown while a storm passed, and the approach to the mast. The film returns to the newsreel for the fire and the count of survivors, 62 of the 97 on board, and ends on the wreck from the air: the end of the passenger airship.
