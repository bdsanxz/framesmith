Thursday, May sixth, nineteen thirty-seven. [Lakehurst](/lˈeɪkhɜːst/), New Jersey.

At seven twenty-one in the evening, the largest aircraft ever built drops its landing lines.

Four minutes later, a flame appears near the tail.

About thirty-four seconds after that, the [Hindenburg](/hˈɪndənbɜːɡ/) is gone.

Until that evening, no Zeppelin passenger had ever been killed.

[Hugo](/hjˈuːɡəʊ/) [Eckener](/ˈɛkənə/), the head of Zeppelin, wanted the ship filled with helium, a gas that cannot burn.

But America holds almost all the world's helium, and the law forbids its export.

So the [Hindenburg](/hˈɪndənbɜːɡ/) flies on seven million cubic feet of hydrogen.

It reaches [Lakehurst](/lˈeɪkhɜːst/) twelve hours late, and circles to wait out a storm. As it nears the mast, the tail catches fire, and the flames run the length of the ship.

Ninety-seven people are on board. Sixty-two get out alive.

The age of the passenger airship ends in a New Jersey field.
