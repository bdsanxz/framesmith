# Krakatoa, Heard Round the World

A 67-second history film in English about the eruption of Krakatoa in the Sunda Strait on 27 August 1883, told through its sound.

It opens on a map that moves in from Indonesia to the strait, shows the volcano erupting since May and the third and largest explosion at 10:02 in the morning, the waves that crossed the strait, and counts the 36,417 dead. It then turns to the sound, the loudest in recorded history: the geologist Rogier Verbeek finds two thirds of the island gone, 165 coastal villages are destroyed, and a diagram of the Indian Ocean follows the sound to Batavia, Perth, and Rodrigues, 4,800 kilometres away, where the chief of police reports the roar of heavy guns. The film returns to the footage for the pressure wave, recorded seven times by the world's barographs, and ends in 1927, when a new island rises from the sea: Anak Krakatau, the child of Krakatoa.
