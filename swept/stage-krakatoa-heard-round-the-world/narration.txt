August twenty-seventh, eighteen eighty-three. [Krakatoa](/kɹˌækətˈOə/), in the [Sunda](/sˈundə/) Strait.

The volcano has rumbled since May. At two minutes past ten, the third and largest explosion tears the island apart.

Waves more than thirty metres high cross the strait.

They kill thirty-six thousand, four hundred and seventeen people.

But what the world remembers is the sound. The loudest sound in recorded history.

[Rogier](/ɹOɡˈɪɹ/) [Verbeek](/vəɹbˈAk/), a Dutch geologist, surveys what is left. Two thirds of the island is gone.

One hundred and sixty-five coastal villages are destroyed.

The sound keeps going. [Batavia](/bətˈAviə/), one hundred and sixty kilometres away. Perth, about three thousand one hundred. [Rodrigues](/ɹɑdɹˈiɡ/), four thousand eight hundred: a police chief reports the roar of heavy guns.

Barographs around the world record the pressure wave seven times.

In nineteen twenty-seven, a new island rises from the sea. [Anak](/ˈɑnɑk/) [Krakatau](/kɹˌækətˈW/): the child of [Krakatoa](/kɹˌækətˈOə/).
